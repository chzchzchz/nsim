Containment isolation phase A has actuated: non-essential penetrations closed to seal the containment boundary. The two-phase design exists because not everything crossing the boundary can close at once - phase A takes the lines that safety does not require open, leaving essential paths for later phases.

Actuation follows containment signals (high pressure or high radiation initiating isolation logic). Afterward, verify closure positions where indicated and account for what stopped flowing through those penetrations - any process relying on them found out at the same moment the alarm did.
