Turbine tripped on low bearing oil pressure - the lubricating header below 15 psi while running. The entire rotating assembly rides on oil films thinner than a human hair; pressure is the only evidence those films exist, and the trip fires well before metal contacts metal. But the trip does not end the oil story - coast-down from rated speed takes many minutes, and every one of them needs lubrication as much as operation did.

Priority after the trip is oil, not diagnosis: confirm the [auxiliary lube oil pump](TurbineBuilding/Turbine/LubeOilPump) is running and [pressure](TurbineBuilding/Turbine/BearingOilPressure) recovered, and keep turning gear engaged through cool-down. Then find why pressure fell - main pump failure, relief lifting, strainer blockage - because the next start inherits whatever broke.

Model note: the 15 psi plate is a simulator-derived typical journal-oil value; the reference names the trip device but states no number.
