Reactor coolant pump shaft speed in rpm. At full speed the pumps circulate their design flow; speed sagging below it means electrical trouble, cavitation from lost suction pressure, or a locked rotor - each with different downstream signatures.

Pump heat is a genuine heat source for the primary. The reference notes primary temperature is maintained through balanced use of RCP heat input and steam dump; at low power or after a trip, running pumps add meaningful energy while stopped ones let the core coast on natural circulation. That cuts both ways: pump heat helps hold no-load temperature during startup, but after a trip it can keep pushing Tavg up unless steam dump carries the load away.

Flow follows speed, and flow sets the Delta-T between hot and cold legs - so speed excursions appear instantly as temperature spread changes.
