Energizes the pressurizer immersion heaters - and to understand why that matters, you need to know what the pressurizer is: a large vessel half full of water, half full of steam, connected to the hot leg. That steam volume is deliberately created during startup and is called the steam bubble. Water is virtually incompressible; without a bubble, every thermal expansion in the primary would spike pressure instantly. With one, the plant has a compressible cushion that absorbs insurges and outsurges while heaters and spray manage pressure around setpoint.

The reference procedure forms the bubble early in heatup: all pressurizer heaters are energized with the plant still near 320-400 psig, heating the trapped water volume until it reaches saturation temperature for that pressure (roughly 428-448F). Charging flow is then reduced while letdown increases, walking level down to about 25 percent as steam replaces water at the top. From that moment on, RCS pressure is controlled by heater and spray operation - heaters raise pressure by boiling more steam, spray lowers it by condensing.
